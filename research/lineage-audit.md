# Leach Lineage Audit

_Last updated: 29 September 2026_

This is the working audit of the maternal Leach line extracted from the Ancestry GEDCOM. The goal is not to preserve every imported hint. The goal is to separate **documented people**, **duplicate profiles**, **probable merges**, and **unproved ancestry**.

## Evidence grades

- **A — strong:** multiple independent records agree on identity and relationships.
- **B — good working conclusion:** records are coherent, but a key primary record still needs to be checked.
- **C — hypothesis:** plausible but not proved.
- **D — conflict:** records actively disagree or appear to combine different people.

## Duplicate cluster: Andrew Milton Leach

The GEDCOM contains at least two records for the same historical Andrew Milton Leach:

### Andrew record A

- born 11 March 1856, Angola, Steuben County, Indiana
- died 17 July 1928, Neosho/Newton County, Missouri
- married Clarissa Emma Mowan
- linked as child of **William Leach Jr.**, born about 1813 in Massachusetts
- comparatively sparse parent data

### Andrew record B

- same birth and death
- same Missouri trajectory
- linked as child of **William Terrill Leach** and **Elizabeth Arnold**
- carries 1870 Indiana, 1880 Missouri, 1900/1910/1920 Missouri census references
- also contains obviously bad attachments, including an 1860 Monroe, Virginia census hit and a Civil War database hit for a man born in 1856

### Audit conclusion

These two records should eventually be merged **only after the parent field is deliberately left unresolved** or independently proved. The shared life events show that the two profiles represent the same Andrew. The parent links do not have equal evidentiary weight simply because they appear in the tree.

## Collision: William Terrill Leach

The imported William Terrill profile shows several signs of a merged identity:

- birth: 20 August 1810, Bridgewater, Massachusetts
- attached arrival records from Liverpool/New York
- attached 1860 residence in Whitley County, Indiana
- death #1: 27 December 1891, Providence, Rhode Island
- death #2: before 1867, Whitley County, Indiana

Two death statements for the same person cannot both be correct.

Independent research on the documented **William Terrill Leach of Bridgewater** identifies him as a son of Peleg and Sally (Gilmore) Leach, husband of Sarah Swift Washburn, living in **Wareham, Massachusetts in both 1850 and 1860**, and dying in Providence in 1891. See:

https://maltbyfamily.net/genealogies/washburn/washburn_plymouth_118.html

That is difficult to reconcile with the imported Indiana father profile. Therefore:

> **Do not currently use William Terrill Leach as Andrew Milton Leach's proved father.**

## The Bridgewater hypothesis

The imported tree continues from William Terrill through:

Peleg → Benjamin → Benjamin → Benjamin → Giles Leach

That may represent a real Bridgewater lineage, but it does not become Derek's lineage unless the **Andrew → father** bridge is proved first.

This repository therefore distinguishes:

1. **the documented Bridgewater Leach family**, which is real;
2. **Derek's documented Missouri/Indiana Leach line**, which is real;
3. **the proposed connection between them**, which remains unproved.

## Giles Leach and Lawrence Leach

Older genealogies frequently call Giles Leach of Bridgewater a son of Lawrence Leach of Salem. Later researchers have challenged that claim.

Useful cautions:

- WeRelate's Giles profile states that there is no credible evidence connecting Giles to Lawrence or Salem: https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Person%3AGiles_Leach_%281%29
- A WikiTree research discussion cites later professional work and Robert Charles Anderson's criticism of early Leach genealogies: https://www.wikitree.com/g2g/69868/conflict-challenge-to-parentage-of-giles-b-leach

**Grade: C/D.** Treat the Giles → Lawrence connection as disputed unless new primary evidence appears.

## Imported-tree contamination already identified

The following items should not be used as proof without re-checking the original record:

- Andrew Milton Leach attached to an 1860 Monroe, Virginia census record despite the coherent Indiana/Missouri life pattern.
- Andrew attached to a Civil War database record even though he was born in 1856 and was about nine when the war ended.
- William Terrill Leach carrying incompatible death places/dates.
- passenger/arrival records attached to Massachusetts-born people without a coherent reason for immigration.
- duplicate people under spelling variants such as **Leach / Leech** and given-name variants such as **Arealis / Aureluss / Bert**.

## Rule going forward

No pedigree jump gets promoted merely because an Ancestry hint, online tree, or 19th-century family genealogy says so. Every generation must fit **chronology + geography + relationships + independent records**.
