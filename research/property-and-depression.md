# Property & Great Depression Research

Family tradition says that the Leach family lost substantial property during the Great Depression. The imported tree does **not yet prove that story**, but it gives us a much tighter place and time to investigate.

## Geographic focus

The direct branch in the imported records moved through:

- **Steuben County, Indiana** — Andrew Milton Leach's birthplace and childhood area
- **Livingston County, Missouri** — Andrew in 1880
- **Barry County, Missouri** — Andrew and later family at Shell Knob
- **Newton County, Missouri** — the family in/around Neosho
- **Otero County, Colorado** — a temporary family residence around 1920
- **Newton County, Missouri** — back in Neosho by 1930
- **Cowley County, Kansas** — Billie Bert and Elmer later in Arkansas City

The Great Depression question should therefore begin in **Barry and Newton Counties, Missouri**, not in colonial Massachusetts.

## Why Newton County is the first target

The imported records place **Arealis "Bert" Leach**, his wife Annie, and **Elmer Edger Leach** in the Neosho area during the Depression years.

Known working timeline:

- 1920 — Bert's household in Fowler, Otero County, Colorado
- 1924 — Elmer married in Neosho
- 1930 — Bert and Elmer households appear in Neosho, Newton County, Missouri
- 1935 — residence references place the family in rural Newton County / Neosho area
- 1940 — Bert still in Neosho; Elmer in Aurora, Lawrence County, Missouri

That makes **1924–1940** the first property-loss window to investigate.

## Records to search

### 1. Deeds

Search grantor/grantee indexes for:

- Arealis Bert Leach
- Bert Leach
- Aureluss Leach
- Leech spelling variants
- Elmer Edger Leach
- Andrew Milton Leach / estate
- Annie Dinah Leach

Record every purchase, sale and quitclaim. A property sale during the Depression is not automatically a financial loss, so each deed must be paired with mortgage and tax evidence where possible.

### 2. Deeds of trust / mortgages

Look for:

- new loans during 1928–1935
- assignments of deeds of trust
- releases/satisfactions
- trustee's deeds after default

A trustee's deed or foreclosure proceeding would be much stronger evidence of involuntary loss than an ordinary warranty deed.

### 3. Delinquent tax lists

County tax records can reveal:

- legal description of the tract
- owner name
- assessed value
- unpaid tax amount
- penalties
- tax judgment
- redemption or failure to redeem

A Depression-era tax sale would fit family tradition particularly well.

### 4. Probate

Andrew died in 1928, right before the Depression. His estate may show:

- land owned at death
- debts and mortgages
- who inherited the land
- whether property was sold to settle the estate

If family land was already under financial pressure in 1928, the Depression may have finished a process that had already begun.

### 5. Newspapers

Search Neosho and Barry County newspapers for:

- sheriff's sales
- trustee's sales
- foreclosure notices
- delinquent-tax notices
- estate sale advertisements
- farm auction notices

## Important distinction

There may be **two different family stories** that have blended together over time:

1. old Massachusetts Leach land allegedly descending from the colonial/Bridgewater line; and
2. land actually owned by Derek's documented Missouri ancestors and lost or sold in the early twentieth century.

Until Andrew's father is resolved, the Missouri property story is the one directly attached to the proven family line.
