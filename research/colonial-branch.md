# Colonial / Bridgewater Branch

This file keeps the older Massachusetts material separate from the proved Missouri/Indiana line until the missing generation at Andrew Milton Leach is resolved.

## The Bridgewater family is real

There is a well-documented Leach family in Bridgewater, Massachusetts. Older published genealogies trace lines through people such as:

- Giles Leach
- Benjamin Leach
- later Benjamin Leaches
- Peleg Leach
- William Terrill Leach

The existence of those people and their Bridgewater relationships is not the same thing as proving that Derek's Andrew Milton Leach descended from them.

## The imported-tree hypothesis

The uploaded GEDCOM proposes:

**Andrew Milton Leach → William Terrill Leach → Peleg Leach → Benjamin line → Giles Leach**

At present the first link is not reliable enough to accept.

## William Terrill problem

The imported tree's William Terrill profile mixes incompatible material. Independent research describes William Terrill Leach of Bridgewater as husband of Sarah Swift Washburn, resident of Wareham, Massachusetts in 1850 and 1860, and deceased in Providence in 1891:

https://maltbyfamily.net/genealogies/washburn/washburn_plymouth_118.html

That conflicts with the same profile being used as Andrew's Indiana father.

## Giles → Lawrence is disputed

Older family histories often state that **Giles Leach of Bridgewater was a son of Lawrence Leach of Salem**. That connection should not currently be presented as proved.

Useful research warnings:

- WeRelate: https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Person%3AGiles_Leach_%281%29
- Source warning on the 1898 genealogy: https://www.werelate.org/wiki/Source%3AChessman%2C_Samuel._Leach_Family_Record
- WikiTree discussion citing later professional research: https://www.wikitree.com/g2g/69868/conflict-challenge-to-parentage-of-giles-b-leach

The core problem is simple: older genealogies repeated the relationship, while later researchers have not found adequate primary evidence establishing Giles as Lawrence's child.

## Lawrence Leach of Salem

Lawrence is a genuine early Massachusetts colonist associated with Salem/Beverly, farming, milling and substantial land. He is historically interesting whether or not he proves to be Derek's ancestor.

For this project, however, Lawrence stays in the **candidate ancestral branch** until both of these bridges are proved:

1. Andrew Milton Leach → Bridgewater line
2. Giles Leach → Lawrence Leach

## Slavery, servitude and captive labor research

The project will not assume that a Leach ancestor owned enslaved people merely because he was wealthy or owned land. Ownership or control of bound labor must be shown by records.

### Records that count as strong evidence

- probate inventories listing an enslaved person as property
- wills bequeathing an enslaved person
- deeds or bills of sale involving an enslaved person
- tax lists explicitly valuing enslaved people
- court cases involving ownership/manumission
- town or church records explicitly identifying an individual as enslaved or a servant for life
- military/captive-distribution records with enough context to establish what happened

### Records requiring caution

Words such as **servant**, **boy**, **girl**, **Negro**, **Indian**, or **captive** can have different legal meanings depending on date and colony/state. The surrounding document must be read before classifying the person's status.

### Current status

The uploaded GEDCOM itself does not contain a reliable direct-line record proving slave ownership. Any slavery/captive-labor findings in collateral Massachusetts branches must therefore be labeled **collateral** until the genealogy connects those people to Derek's direct line.

## Research principle

The point of this repository is to reconstruct what actually happened. If a record shows wealth, slavery, indenture, captivity, poverty, migration, land loss, or none of those things, it gets recorded as found rather than bent to fit the family story.
