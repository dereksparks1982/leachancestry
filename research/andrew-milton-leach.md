# Case File: Andrew Milton Leach (1856–1928)

Andrew is currently the most important person in the project because his identity is reasonably coherent while his **parentage is not**.

**Working status (29 Sep 2026):** Andrew's father appears to have been a Massachusetts-born Leach, but the imported identification of that man as **William Terrill Leach of Bridgewater** is not supported. Andrew's own 1910 census entry says his father was born in Massachusetts and his mother in Pennsylvania. That new evidence also conflicts with the imported mother **Elizabeth Arnold**, whose profile says she was born in Ohio.

## Stable life pattern

The imported records consistently point to one man:

- **Name:** Andrew Milton Leach
- **Born:** 11 March 1856
- **Birthplace:** Angola / Steuben County, Indiana
- **Marriage:** 18 August 1874, Kosciusko County, Indiana, to Clarissa Emma Mowan
- **1870:** Scott Township, Steuben County, Indiana
- **1880:** Sampsel, Livingston County, Missouri
- **1900:** Shell Knob, Barry County, Missouri
- **1910:** Shell Knob, Barry County, Missouri
- **1920:** Shell Knob, Barry County, Missouri
- **Died:** 17 July 1928, Newton County / Neosho area, Missouri
- **Burial:** Shell Knob, Barry County, Missouri

The Ancestry GEDCOM includes references to federal census records, Missouri state material, a death certificate collection, a Neosho newspaper death item, FamilySearch and Find a Grave.

## New evidence: Andrew's reported parental birthplaces

A Barry County, Missouri transcription of the **1910 federal census** gives this household in Shell Knob Township:

- **A. M. Leach**, male, 54, born Indiana
- **father born Massachusetts**
- **mother born Pennsylvania**
- **Clarissa Leach**, wife, 57, born Ohio

Source: Missouri GenWeb, 1910 Barry County census abstract: https://www.mogenweb.org/barry/census/1910sk-4-6.htm

This is presently one of the strongest clues to Andrew's parentage because the information was recorded while Andrew himself was alive. It does not prove the identities of his parents, but it gives a concrete test for candidate parents.

### Consequence for the imported Elizabeth Arnold link

The GEDCOM's Elizabeth Arnold profile says she was born **21 March 1831 in Cincinnati, Ohio**. If the 1910 census information is correct, a Pennsylvania-born woman should be sought as Andrew's mother instead. Census birthplace answers can be wrong, so Elizabeth is not excluded solely by this one entry, but her identification is now **weaker rather than stronger**.

## New evidence: exact first-marriage location

The GEDCOM contains FamilySearch references for Andrew and Clarissa's marriage and places it on:

- **18 August 1874**
- **Kosciusko County, Indiana**

FamilySearch record identifiers preserved in the GEDCOM:

- Andrew: `VRQQ-QSV`
- Clarissa: `VRQQ-QSJ`

The original marriage return/license should be obtained and transcribed. It may establish residence, age, officiant, witnesses or other associates even if it does not name Andrew's parents.

## The two competing parent links in the imported GEDCOM

### Candidate 1: William Leach Jr.

One duplicate Andrew profile gives:

- **William Leach Jr.**
- born about 1813, Massachusetts
- died before 1867, Whitley County, Indiana

This profile is extremely thin. Its only attached source is an 1843 New York passenger-arrival record, which does not establish that the passenger was the Indiana man or Andrew's father. However, the candidate's stated **Massachusetts birth** is consistent with Andrew's 1910 census answer, so the identity should be investigated rather than discarded.

The GEDCOM also gives this William a father called simply **William Leach Sr.**, with no useful identifying information. That paternal link is presently unsupported.

### Candidate 2: William Terrill Leach + Elizabeth Arnold

The second Andrew profile gives:

- **William Terrill Leach**, born 20 Aug 1810 in Bridgewater, Massachusetts
- **Elizabeth Arnold**, born 21 Mar 1831 in Ohio
- marriage in the imported family record: 24 Apr 1850, Wabash, Indiana
- children listed as Sylvester, Sylvanus A., Andrew Milton and Amza M. Ann

The problem is that the imported William Terrill record is internally contradictory and appears to combine two different men.

## Why William Terrill is currently rejected as a proved father

Independent genealogical work identifies William Terrill Leach of Bridgewater as:

- son of **Peleg Leach and Sally Gilmore**;
- husband of **Sarah Swift Washburn**;
- married 2 August 1835 in Wareham, Massachusetts;
- resident of Wareham in the 1850 and 1860 federal censuses;
- a machinist;
- died 27 December 1891 in Providence, Rhode Island.

Source: https://maltbyfamily.net/genealogies/washburn/washburn_plymouth_118.html

That documented Massachusetts trajectory conflicts sharply with the imported claim that the same man married Elizabeth Arnold in Indiana and fathered Andrew there.

The GEDCOM itself gives William Terrill two incompatible death entries:

- 27 Dec 1891, Providence, Rhode Island
- before 1867, Whitley County, Indiana

It also attaches an **1860 Cleveland Township, Whitley County, Indiana** census entry to this profile while the documented Bridgewater William Terrill was living in Wareham, Massachusetts. That is strong evidence of a profile collision or bad merge.

The alleged 24 April 1850 Wabash County marriage to Elizabeth Arnold also needs the original county record. The public Indiana State Library database explains that its pre-1851 marriage index is a compiled index of courthouse records and warns that transcription errors can occur. Until the original license/return is found, this marriage should not be used to connect Andrew to the Bridgewater family.

Indiana State Library index description: https://digital.statelib.lib.in.us/legacy/search.aspx?index=3

## A possible sibling clue that also needs separation

The imported William-Terrill/Elizabeth family includes a **Sylvanus A. Leach**, born about 1855 in Indiana. A separate burial index places a **Sylvanus Leach (1855–1889)** in Spring Creek Cemetery at Sidney, Indiana. The matching year is interesting, but it is not enough to establish that this was Andrew's brother. This should be treated as an identity problem, not merged automatically.

## Bad attachments on Andrew's duplicate profile

The larger Andrew profile contains records that should be removed from consideration unless the original images prove otherwise:

- **1860 Monroe, Virginia census** — geographically inconsistent with the otherwise coherent Indiana childhood.
- **American Civil War Research Database** — Andrew was born in 1856 and would have been a child during the war.

These bad hints demonstrate why source count by itself is not evidence quality.

## Best next record: 1928 death certificate

The Missouri State Archives states that death certificates can provide date of birth, parents' names, spouse, occupation, funeral home and burial information. It also states that digitized images of original certificates are linked from its database search results.

Database: https://s1.sos.mo.gov/records/Archives/ArchivesMvc/DeathCertificates

The imported GEDCOM points to the Missouri Death Certificates collection for Andrew (`Ancestry collection record 1888876`), so the actual image should be retrieved and transcribed. The parent names on that certificate will be important, but they must still be evaluated according to **who supplied the information**.

The GEDCOM also preserves a Neosho Daily News obituary/index reference dated **17 July 1928**, the day of Andrew's death. The underlying newspaper item is another high-priority target because it may identify siblings, birthplace, children or other relatives.

## Census reconstruction needed

### 1870

Imported citation:

- Scott Township, Steuben County, Indiana
- page 193B
- post office Angola
- NARA roll M593-359

Independent references to neighboring pages confirm that page 193B is genuinely within the Scott Township enumeration, so this citation is geographically plausible. The remaining task is to transcribe Andrew's complete household and neighboring households.

### 1860

The imported Virginia census attachment should be treated as a false hint. Instead, search Steuben, Whitley, Kosciusko, Wabash and adjacent Indiana counties for:

- Andrew Leach, age about 4
- a father born in Massachusetts
- a mother likely born in Pennsylvania
- William Leach candidates
- possible siblings only after their identities are independently established

The GEDCOM's William Terrill profile cites an 1860 Cleveland Township, Whitley County household on **page 894, roll M653-307**. The original page must be transcribed because the man in that household may be the real Indiana William who was accidentally merged into William Terrill of Bridgewater.

## Working hypothesis

The evidence currently fits this possibility best:

> There was a **different William Leach, born in Massachusetts around 1813**, living in northern Indiana, and later Ancestry trees merged him with William Terrill Leach of Bridgewater because their names, ages and Massachusetts origins looked similar.

That is a hypothesis, not a conclusion. The decisive records are the 1860 Indiana household, Andrew's 1870 household, Andrew's 1928 death certificate, and any probate/guardianship record created if the Indiana William died while Andrew was still a minor.

## Decision rule

Andrew's father will be promoted only when at least two strong pieces of evidence converge, ideally:

1. a primary or near-primary record naming the parent; and
2. a household/probate/marriage/deed record placing the same family together.

DNA cousin matches can then be used as corroboration rather than as a substitute for documentary genealogy.
